The development of the Dual-Ratio Transmission centered around a co-designed mechanism that utilized one-way roller clutches to shift gear ratios by reversing the motor direction. The drivetrain design was validated before manufacturing using MATLAB, hand calculations, and ANSYS Mechanical FEA to optimize gear ratios, refine shift timing, and determine shaft geometry (which accounted for frictional losses and shaft deformation under load). Following the design and validation phase, we moved into production, involving engineering drawing creation, component machining, and complete assembly of the drivetrain.

During prototyping, testing was required to evaluate the shifting mechanism. I managed the electromechanical integration for these initial tests by wiring a Double-Pole Double-Throw (DPDT) switch for polarity reversal, connecting the motor directly to the switch's two middle pins. This allowed us to trigger the motor reversal required for shifting. However, physical testing revealed slip between the aluminum and acetal components. To resolve this, we added aluminum plates and hubs to the acetal gears, enabling a metal-on-metal press fit onto the roller clutches, eliminating the slipping issue.

Just before the competition, we determined that top-end performance needed enhancement, prompting a change in the high-speed gear ratio from 5.04:1 to 4:1. Since the transmission utilized 3D-printed pulleys, we could switch pulley sizes quickly to accommodate this ratio change without manufacturing delays. Ultimately, this drivetrain design improved the vehicle's overall acceleration by 18%, allowing the team to win 1st place and set a competition record.
